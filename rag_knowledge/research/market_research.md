# Market Research Standards

## 1. Purpose

Market research must provide reliable information about the target market, customers, industry, trends, opportunities, and risks.

The research should support product and business decisions rather than simply collecting information.

## 2. Research Objectives

Before starting research, clearly define:

- What problem is being investigated?
- What market or industry is being analyzed?
- Who are the target customers?
- What decision will the research support?
- What information is required?
- What timeframe is relevant?

## 3. Market Definition

Clearly identify:

- Target market
- Industry
- Geographic region
- Customer segment
- Product category
- Market maturity
- Relevant business model

Avoid making assumptions about the market without supporting evidence.

## 4. Customer Research

Analyze the target customers based on:

- Customer needs
- Pain points
- Existing solutions
- Buying behavior
- Budget considerations
- User expectations
- Adoption barriers
- Customer priorities

Separate verified customer information from assumptions.

## 5. Market Size

Where possible, estimate:

- Total Addressable Market (TAM)
- Serviceable Addressable Market (SAM)
- Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM)

Clearly identify the source and assumptions behind each estimate.

## 6. Market Trends

Research relevant trends such as:

- Technology adoption
- Customer behavior
- Industry growth
- Regulatory changes
- Emerging technologies
- Business-model changes
- Economic factors

Each important trend should have supporting evidence.

## 7. Research Sources

Prefer reliable sources such as:

- Government publications
- Official industry reports
- Company annual reports
- Official company websites
- Reputable research organizations
- Academic publications
- Established technology publications
- Credible market research reports

Avoid relying on a single source for important conclusions.

## 8. Source Validation

Before using information:

1. Identify the source.
2. Check the publication date.
3. Check the credibility of the source.
4. Compare important claims with other sources.
5. Determine whether the information is current.
6. Record the source for traceability.

Do not treat search-engine summaries as primary evidence.

## 9. Data Quality

Classify information appropriately:

```text
Verified Fact
     ↓
Strong Evidence
     ↓
Reasonable Inference
     ↓
Assumption

Do not present assumptions as verified facts.

10. Competitor Information

When researching competitors, collect information such as:

Product offering
Target customers
Pricing
Key features
Technology
Business model
Market positioning
Strengths
Weaknesses
Customer feedback
Differentiators

Competitor information should be based on publicly available and reliable sources.

11. Quantitative Analysis

When using numerical data:

Record the source.
Record the date.
Explain important assumptions.
Verify calculations.
Use consistent units.
Distinguish estimates from reported values.

Do not manufacture missing statistics.

12. Qualitative Analysis

Qualitative information should identify:

Common customer problems
Repeated themes
User expectations
Industry concerns
Adoption barriers
Emerging opportunities

Opinions should be clearly identified as opinions.

13. AI-Assisted Research

AI agents may assist with:

Information discovery
Source summarization
Data classification
Trend identification
Competitor comparison
Report generation

AI-generated research must not automatically be treated as fact.

Important claims must be validated against reliable sources.

14. Research Agent Standards

The research agent should:

Understand the research objective.
Break the objective into research questions.
Gather relevant information.
Validate important sources.
Remove irrelevant information.
Identify conflicting information.
Separate facts from assumptions.
Summarize findings.
Provide source references.
Highlight uncertainty.

16. Recommendations

Recommendations must be connected to research findings.

Use:

Finding → Evidence → Implication → Recommendation

Avoid recommendations based solely on assumptions or personal opinions.

17. Research Freshness

Time-sensitive information should be checked for recency.

Examples include:

Pricing
Product features
Market share
Competitor offerings
Technology availability
Regulations
Company status

Older information should be clearly identified when it is still relevant.

18. Bias Prevention

Research should avoid:

Confirmation bias
Cherry-picking evidence
Unsupported assumptions
Overreliance on one source
Favoring a preferred product or company
Treating marketing claims as independent evidence

Consider both supporting and contradicting evidence.

19. Confidentiality

Do not expose confidential company information during external research.

Never send:

Internal credentials
API keys
Private customer information
Confidential business documents
Internal architecture details
Proprietary source code

to external research services unless explicitly authorized.

20. Research Completion Criteria

Market research is considered complete when:

 Research objective is clearly defined.
 Target market is identified.
 Customer segment is identified.
 Relevant trends are analyzed.
 Market-size information is supported.
 Competitors are identified where applicable.
 Important claims have reliable sources.
 Facts and assumptions are separated.
 Conflicting evidence is considered.
 Findings are summarized.
 Recommendations are evidence-based.
 Sources are recorded.
21. Agentic SDLC Rule

Market research should provide sufficient evidence for downstream agents.

Market Research
       ↓
Validated Findings
       ↓
Requirements Agent
       ↓
Design Agent
       ↓
Development Agents

Downstream agents must not treat unsupported market assumptions as confirmed requirements.
