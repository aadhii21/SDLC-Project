# Competitor Analysis Standards

## 1. Purpose

Competitor analysis must identify, evaluate, and compare competing products, services, companies, and solutions.

The analysis should help the business understand the competitive landscape and identify opportunities for differentiation.

## 2. Analysis Objectives

Before starting competitor analysis, clearly define:

- What product or service is being analyzed?
- Who are the target customers?
- Which competitors are relevant?
- What geographic market is being considered?
- What aspects of competitors need to be compared?
- What business decision will the analysis support?

## 3. Competitor Identification

Identify competitors based on:

- Direct competitors
- Indirect competitors
- Substitute solutions
- Emerging competitors
- Open-source alternatives
- Internal or existing customer alternatives

Do not assume that only companies offering identical products are competitors.

## 4. Competitor Information

Where applicable, collect:

- Company overview
- Product offerings
- Target customers
- Pricing
- Key features
- Technology
- Business model
- Market positioning
- Geographic presence
- Strengths
- Weaknesses
- Differentiators
- Customer feedback
- Recent product developments

## 5. Source Standards

Prefer reliable sources such as:

- Official company websites
- Official product documentation
- Company annual reports
- Investor reports
- Government or regulatory sources
- Reputable industry reports
- Academic publications
- Established technology publications
- Verified customer reviews

Do not treat competitor marketing claims as independently verified facts.

## 6. Competitor Comparison

Use consistent evaluation criteria for all competitors.

Example:

| Security | | | |

| Strengths | | | |
| Weaknesses | | | |

Do not compare competitors using different criteria.

## 7. Product Comparison

Analyze important product capabilities such as:

- Core functionality
- User experience
- Performance
- Reliability
- Integrations
- Customization
- Automation
- AI capabilities
- Security
- Support
- Pricing

Only mark a feature as supported when reliable evidence exists.

## 8. Pricing Analysis

When analyzing pricing:

- Record the source.
- Record the date.
- Identify the pricing model.
- Distinguish free and paid features.
- Identify usage-based pricing where applicable.
- Identify enterprise pricing where publicly available.
- Clearly label estimates.

Do not invent unavailable pricing information.

10. Competitive Advantages

Identify areas where competitors may have advantages such as:

Lower pricing
Better technology
Better user experience
Larger ecosystem
Stronger brand
Better integrations
Higher scalability
Better customer support
Greater market presence

Each important advantage should have supporting evidence.

11. Competitive Gaps

Identify potential gaps such as:

Missing features
Poor user experience
High pricing
Limited integrations
Poor automation
Scalability limitations
Security limitations
Underserved customer segments

Competitive gaps must be based on evidence rather than assumptions.

12. Customer Feedback

When analyzing customer feedback:

Look for repeated patterns.
Separate individual opinions from common trends.
Consider both positive and negative feedback.
Avoid treating a small number of reviews as representative of the entire customer base.
Record the source and date where possible.
13. Technology Analysis

When technology information is publicly available, evaluate:

Technology stack
AI/LLM usage
Cloud infrastructure
APIs
Integrations
Architecture characteristics
Automation capabilities
Scalability

Do not claim knowledge of proprietary internal architecture without reliable evidence.

14. AI-Assisted Competitor Analysis

AI agents may assist with:

Competitor discovery
Information extraction
Feature comparison
Pricing comparison
SWOT analysis
Trend identification
Review analysis
Report generation

AI-generated competitor information must be validated against reliable sources.

15. Competitor Analysis Agent

The competitor analysis agent should:

Understand the target product.
Identify relevant competitors.
Collect reliable information.
Validate important claims.
Compare competitors using consistent criteria.
Identify strengths and weaknesses.
Identify competitive gaps.
Analyze market positioning.
Highlight opportunities and risks.
Produce an evidence-based summary.
16. Avoid Competitor Bias

Do not:

Favor a competitor without evidence.
Assume a competitor is better because it is larger.
Assume a smaller competitor is inferior.
Rely on a single review.
Copy competitor claims without validation.
Make unsupported statements about competitor technology.
Present assumptions as facts.
17. Competitive Positioning

The analysis should identify where the proposed product can compete based on:

Price
Features
Quality
Performance
Customer experience
Automation
Technology
Security
Support
Differentiation

The goal is to identify a defensible competitive position.

18. Research Freshness

Competitor information can change frequently.

Verify current information for:

Pricing
Product features
Product availability
Company status
Technology capabilities
Market positioning
Partnerships
Product launches

Always consider the publication date of important information.

19. Confidentiality

Competitor analysis must use legally and ethically obtained information.

Do not:

Attempt to access private systems.
Obtain confidential competitor information.
Use stolen credentials.
Circumvent access controls.
Expose confidential information.

Use publicly available and authorized information.

21. Recommendation Standards

Recommendations must connect competitive evidence to business decisions.

Use:

Competitive Finding
        ↓
Evidence
        ↓
Business Implication
        ↓
Opportunity
        ↓
Recommendation

Avoid recommendations based only on assumptions.

22. Competitor Analysis Checklist

Before completing the analysis:

 Objective is clearly defined.
 Relevant competitors are identified.
 Direct and indirect competitors are considered.
 Competitor information is sourced.
 Features are compared consistently.
 Pricing information is validated.
 Strengths and weaknesses are identified.
 Competitive gaps are identified.
 Customer feedback is considered where relevant.
 Technology claims are validated.
 Facts and assumptions are separated.
 Current information is used where required.
 Recommendations are evidence-based.
 Sources are recorded.
23. Agentic SDLC Rule

Competitor analysis should provide validated competitive insights to downstream agents.

Competitor Analysis
        ↓
